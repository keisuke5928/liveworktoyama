title: City office and immigration notices after moving
description: What to do at the city office after you move to Toyama (address registration within 14 days, My Number, health insurance and pension), which changes you must report to immigration, and what to know about your residence card.
---
## What do I need to do at the city office after I move?
1. Register your address at the city office within 14 days after you move in. Bring your residence card. Your new address is written on the back.
2. In Toyama City, the Citizens Section (Shimin-ka) is on the 1st floor of City Hall. Your company or school may help you with the first visit.
3. When you move to another city, report your move-out at the old city office and your move-in at the new one. Within the same city, one moving notice is enough.
4. You can apply for a My Number card. It is used for taxes, insurance, health checks and many online services. It also works as your health insurance card.
5. If your company does not give you health insurance and pension, join National Health Insurance and the National Pension at the city office.
6. Ask for the garbage calendar and information for foreign residents.
7. Get a copy of your resident record (juminhyo) if your company, bank or phone shop asks for it.

## What do I need to report to the Immigration Services Agency?
1. If you change or leave your company or school, many visa types must report this to immigration within 14 days. You can do it online.
2. Your residence card has an expiry date. You can usually apply to extend your period of stay from about 3 months before it ends.
3. For questions about your own visa, ask immigration or a gyoseishoshi.

## What should I know about my residence card?
1. If you are 16 or older, always carry your residence card.
2. If you lose it, apply for a new card at immigration within 14 days after you notice. Bring your passport, a photo (4 cm x 3 cm) and a paper that shows why you lost it, such as a lost property report from the police.
3. When you leave Japan for good, return your card at the airport. See our guide [Leaving Japan](/en/guides/leaving-japan/).
