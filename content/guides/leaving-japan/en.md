title: Leaving Japan: pension refund, tax and other steps
description: What to do before you leave Japan: move-out notice, returning your residence card, the pension lump-sum withdrawal payment, resident tax, bank and phone.
---
When you leave Japan for good, some steps must be done before you go, and some after. If you plan to come back, check the re-entry rules first.

## What if I will come back to Japan?
1. If you come back within 1 year (and before your period of stay ends), you can use special re-entry permission. Show your passport and residence card, and fill in the re-entry part of the ED card when you leave.
2. If you will be away for more than 1 year, apply for a re-entry permit at immigration before you leave.

## What do I do before I leave for good?
1. Submit a move-out notice (tenshutsu todoke) at your city office.
2. Return your residence card to the immigration officer at the airport or port.
3. Cancel your phone contract and close your bank accounts. Never sell or give them to anyone.
4. Do not leave garbage or furniture in your room.

## Do I still pay resident tax?
1. Resident tax is charged by the city where you lived on January 1. You must pay it even if you leave Japan after January 2.
2. If your company takes it from your salary, ask the company to take the remaining amount from your last salary or severance pay, or pay it yourself.
3. If you cannot pay it before you leave, choose a tax agent who lives in Japan and tell the city office.

## Can I get my pension payments back?
1. You may get a Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment if all of these are true: you do not have Japanese nationality; you paid into the Employees' Pension or the National Pension for 6 months or more; your total pension period is less than 10 years; you no longer have an address in Japan; and you never had the right to a Japanese pension.
2. Apply within 2 years after you no longer have an address in Japan.
3. The amount is based on up to 5 years (60 months) of payments.
4. Your application is rejected if you still have an address in Japan when it arrives. Submit the move-out notice first.
5. After you receive it, those pension periods no longer count. If you might get a Japanese pension later, think carefully first.
6. Japan has social security agreements with some countries. Your periods may be added together with your home country.
