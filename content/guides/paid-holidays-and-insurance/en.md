title: Paid holidays and insurance
description: Paid holidays (yukyu), health insurance, work injury insurance (rosai) and unemployment benefits in Japan, explained simply for foreign workers in Toyama.
---
## How many paid holidays do I get?
1. After 6 months of work with good attendance (80% or more of work days), full-time workers get at least 10 days of paid holiday a year. The number goes up each year.
2. Part-time workers also get paid holidays, based on how many days they work.
3. Tell your company early when you want to take a day off.
4. Unused days can be carried over to the next year, then they expire.
5. The number of days goes up to a maximum of 20 days a year.
6. If you get 10 or more days a year, your company must make sure you take at least 5 of them.
7. You can usually take paid holidays when you want, for any reason. For more work rules, see [Your rights at work](/en/guides/rights-at-work/).

## How does health insurance work?
1. Show your insurance card (or My Number card) at hospitals. You usually pay 30% of the cost.
2. If medical costs in one month are very high, you may get part of the money back.

## What if I get injured at work?
1. If you are injured at work or on your way to work, tell your company right away.
2. Work injury insurance (rosai) covers treatment and part of your pay. Do not use your normal health insurance card for a work injury.
3. Your company cannot fire you because you were injured at work and are taking time off to recover.

## What if I lose my job?
1. If you had employment insurance, you may get unemployment benefits. Ask at Hello Work.
2. Many visas must report leaving a company to immigration within 14 days.

## How can I avoid paying a lot at the hospital at once?
1. Japan has a limit on how much you pay for medical care in one month (high-cost medical care benefit). The limit depends on your income.
2. If you show your My Number card as your health insurance card, the hospital uses the limit automatically, so you only pay up to the limit.
3. Without it, you can ask your health insurance for a "limit certificate" (gendogaku tekiyo ninteisho) before you go into hospital.
4. Meals in hospital and private rooms are not included.
