title: Your rights at work: hours, overtime and quitting
description: Basic Japanese work rules for foreign workers in Toyama: working hours, breaks, days off, overtime pay rates, how wages must be paid, quitting and dismissal.
---
Japanese labor law protects every worker, including foreign workers, part-time workers and dispatched workers. These are the basic rules.

## How long can I be asked to work?
1. The legal limit is 8 hours a day and 40 hours a week.
2. If you work more than 6 hours in a day, you get a break of at least 45 minutes. More than 8 hours: at least 60 minutes.
3. You get at least 1 day off a week, or at least 4 days off in 4 weeks.
4. Overtime is only allowed if the company has a written agreement with the workers (called a "36 agreement"). In principle, overtime is up to 45 hours a month and 360 hours a year.

## How much extra do I get for overtime?
1. Overtime beyond 8 hours a day or 40 hours a week: at least 25% more.
2. Overtime of more than 60 hours in a month: at least 50% more for those hours.
3. Work on a legal day off: at least 35% more.
4. Night work from 22:00 to 5:00: at least 25% more. Overtime at night: at least 50% more.
5. These rates apply to all workers, including part-time and dispatched workers.

## How must my wages be paid?
1. In money, directly to you, and in full. A bank transfer is allowed if you agree.
2. At least once a month, on a fixed date.
3. Only legal deductions (tax, insurance) and deductions agreed with the workers' representative can be taken out.
4. Your pay can never be below the minimum wage, even if you agreed to a lower amount.
5. If the company tells you to stay home for its own reasons, it must pay at least 60% of your average wage.

## What can a company not do?
1. Set a fine in your contract for breaking the contract.
2. Lend you money and then take it from your salary without your agreement.
3. Force you to save money through the company.
4. If your real working conditions are different from what was promised, you can cancel the contract right away.

## How do I quit my job?
1. Check your company's rules and tell your supervisor early. It is best to give notice in writing.
2. Contract without a fixed end date: the contract ends 2 weeks after you give notice.
3. Fixed-term contract: in principle you cannot quit in the middle without an unavoidable reason. After one year from the start of the contract, you can quit at any time with notice.
4. Many statuses of residence must report leaving a company to immigration within 14 days.

## Can my company fire me?
1. Not without an objective and reasonable reason.
2. The company must tell you at least 30 days before, or pay at least 30 days of average wages.
3. If your company goes bankrupt and cannot pay your wages, part of the unpaid wages may be paid by the government. Ask the Labour Standards Inspection Office.

## Where can I get help?
1. First talk to your company or your support organization.
2. The Labour Standards Inspection Office and the phone lines for foreign workers can help in many languages.

## Where can I talk about work problems in Toyama?
1. General labor consultation corners give free advice about dismissal, pay cuts, harassment and other problems, in person or by phone.
2. Toyama: 076-415-8733 (inside the Toyama Labour Standards Inspection Office, 1-5-5 Jinzu-honmachi, Toyama City).
3. Takaoka: 0766-23-6480. Uozu: 0765-22-0579. Tonami: 0763-32-3323.
4. Hours: weekdays 9:30 to 16:30 (except 12:00 to 13:00). No appointment needed, but calling first is recommended.
5. The consultation is in Japanese. Ask the Toyama Foreign Resident Support Center (076-441-6330) for interpreting by phone.
